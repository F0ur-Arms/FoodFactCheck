Terbinafine (Lamisil)
Terbinafine is a medicine used to treat fungal infections. You can buy it from shops and pharmacies, but some types are only available with a prescription.
What terbinafine is for
Terbinafine is an antifungal medicine. It's used to treat:
skin infections, such as athlete's foot, ringworm, pityriasis versicolor, or jock itch (around the groin, top of the legs and buttocks)
nail infections
How to take terbinafine and how much to take
Terbinafine usually comes as cream, gel or spray you put on your skin, or as tablets you swallow.
Which type you need and how long you use it for depends on why you're using it.
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
How to use or take terbinafine
Cream, gel or spray
You'll usually need to apply terbinafine cream, gel or spray to the affected area once or twice a day for a few weeks.
If you're using it for athlete's foot, you can also get a product that only needs a single (one-time) application.
When using terbinafine cream, gel or spray:
make sure the affected area is clean and dry
apply the cream, gel or spray to the affected area – check the packet or leaflet to see how much to use and for how long
if you're using the cream or gel, gently rub it into your skin
do not use it on your face, and avoid getting it in your eyes, mouth or on any broken skin
wash your hands after using it
Tablets
You'll usually need to take terbinafine tablets once a day for a few weeks or months.
Your doctor will explain your dose to you and tell you how long you need to take them for.
When taking terbinafine tablets:
swallow them whole with a drink of water
try to take it at the same time each day
you can take them with or without food
Always take or use terbinafine for as long as the instructions, or your pharmacist or doctor, tell you.
If you do not use it for long enough, it may not work or the infection may come back.
If your symptoms have not improved or have got worse after using terbinafine cream, gel or spray for 2 weeks, speak to your pharmacist or doctor.
If you miss a dose of terbinafine
If you're taking terbinafine tablets and forget to take a dose, take it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for your next dose.
If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
If you forget a dose of terbinafine cream, use it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for the next application. If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the one you missed and carry on applying it as usual.
Do not take 2 doses of terbinafine to make up for a missed dose.
If you take or use too much terbinafine
Do not worry if you accidentally use more terbinafine cream, gel or spray than you should, or have taken an extra dose of terbinafine tablets. It's unlikely to cause problems, but avoid doing it too often as it can increase the chance of side effects.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of terbinafine tablets and it's causing any symptoms
you've accidentally swallowed terbinafine cream, gel or spray and it's causing any symptoms

Side effects of terbinafine
Like all medicines, terbinafine can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Possible side effects of terbinafine cream, gel or spray include:
itchy, irritated or peeling skin
pain or a burning feeling on your skin
redness or other changes to your skin colour (this may be harder to see on black or brown skin)
Possible side effects of terbinafine tablets include:
headache
diarrhoea
a rash
joint or muscle pain
stomach ache, indigestion, feeling bloated or not feeling as hungry as usual
loss of taste or a change in how things taste
feeling dizzy
Terbinafine will not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery, but if you feel dizzy or very tired after taking it, wait until this stops.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Terbinafine can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of terbinafine include:
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash and difficulty breathing
a severe skin reaction (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) – causing flu-like symptoms, a rash, blisters and sores
problems with your blood – causing symptoms such as a sore throat, sudden high temperature (you may feel hot, cold or shivery), bruising easily, nosebleeds or bleeding gums, feeling breathless, or small red or purple spots on your skin (this may be harder to see on black or brown skin)
pancreatitis – causing symptoms such as severe pain in the upper part of your tummy, which may spread to your side and back
liver problems – causing symptoms such as itchy skin, darker pee than normal, pale poo and yellowing of the eyes or skin (this may be harder to see on black or brown skin)
If you're taking terbinafine tablets, you will need regular blood tests to monitor your liver.
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking terbinafine and think you're having symptoms of a severe skin reaction or anaphylaxis
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

Who can and cannot take or use terbinafine
Most adults and children aged 16 and over can take or use terbinafine. It's only available on prescription for children under 16.
But terbinafine is not suitable for some people.
For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from terbinafine or other antifungal medicine.
Terbinafine tablets may also not be suitable if:
you have severe liver problems or kidney problems
you have psoriasis or lupus
you have joint pain
Check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine to see if it's suitable for you. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you're not sure if you can take or use it.
Breastfeeding and terbinafine
Terbinafine is not usually recommended if you're breastfeeding. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor for advice.
Pregnancy and terbinafine
Speak to a doctor or pharmacist before taking or using terbinafine if you're pregnant or think you might be pregnant. They may suggest a different medicine.
Terbinafine is not usually recommended in pregnancy but may be used if the benefits outweigh the risks.
Taking terbinafine with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with terbinafine.
If you're taking terbinafine tablets, this includes:
antidepressants
other medicines used to treat fungal infections, such fluconazole
medicines containing caffeine, such as some cold remedies or painkillers
medicines for heart problems, such as beta blockers or anti-arrhythmics (like amiodarone)
opioid painkillers, such as codeine or tramadol
Check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine to see if it's suitable for you. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you're not sure you can take it.
Food, drink and alcohol with terbinafine
It's best not to drink too much caffeine while taking terbinafine tablets. The medicine can increase the effects of caffeine.
Otherwise, you can eat and drink as normal.
