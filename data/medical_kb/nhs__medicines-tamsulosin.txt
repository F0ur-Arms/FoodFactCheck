Tamsulosin
Tamsulosin is a medicine used to treat symptoms of an enlarged prostate. It's only available with a prescription.
What tamsulosin is for
Tamsulosin is mainly used to treat difficulty peeing caused by an enlarged prostate.
It's sometimes also used to treat kidney stones.
It works by relaxing the muscles around the prostate and bladder, which can make it easier to pee.
How to take tamsulosin
Tamsulosin comes as tablets or capsules. Swallow the tablet or capsule whole with water – do not crush or chew it.
You usually need to take tamsulosin once a day, with or without food.
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
If you miss a dose of tamsulosin
If you miss a dose of tamsulosin, take it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for your next dose.
If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses to make up for a missed dose.
If you take too much tamsulosin
Taking an extra dose of tamsulosin can be dangerous.
Urgent advice: Call NHS 111 if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of tamsulosin
If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.
Bring the tamsulosin packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.
Side effects of tamsulosin
Like all medicines, tamsulosin can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of tamsulosin include:
dizziness, particularly when you sit down or stand up
ejaculation problems
If you feel dizzy after taking tamsulosin, do not drive, ride a bike or use machinery until you no longer have these symptoms.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Tamsulosin can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of tamsulosin include:
fainting
a long-lasting, painful erection (priapism)
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – which can cause symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
a severe skin reaction (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) – causing flu-like symptoms, a rash, blisters and sores
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking tamsulosin and think you might be having anaphylaxis or Stevens-Johnson syndrome
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.
More about side effects
These are not all the side effects of tamsulosin.
For a full list, see the leaflet that comes with your medicine or search for your medicine leaflet on the electronic medicines compendium website.
You can report any side effects you get on the Yellow Card website
Who can and cannot take tamsulosin
Most adults can take tamsulosin.
It's also sometimes prescribed to children, but this is rare.
Tamsulosin is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from tamsulosin or a similar medicine
you have liver or kidney problems
you've ever fainted during or after peeing
you've been told you have orthostatic hypotension (you get dizzy and light-headed when you stand up)
you're about to have eye surgery
Before taking tamsulosin, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and tamsulosin
Tell your doctor if you're breastfeeding. Tamsulosin is not usually used if you're breastfeeding.
Pregnancy and tamsulosin
Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or trying to get pregnant. Tamsulosin is not usually used during pregnancy.
Find out more about taking tamsulosin during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy) website
Taking tamsulosin with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with tamsulosin.
These include:
some blood pressure medicines, such as doxazosin and diltiazem
some antibiotics, such as erythromycin
some medicines to treat fungal infections
medicines to treat erectile dysfunction, like sildenafil (Viagra)
medicines to treat HIV
Before taking tamsulosin, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with tamsulosin
You can eat and drink as normal while taking tamsulosin, but it's best not to drink alcohol as it can increase the risk of side effects.
