Nitrofurantoin
Nitrofurantoin is an antibiotic used to treat urinary tract infections (UTIs) such as cystitis. It's only available with a prescription
What nitrofurantoin is for
Nitrofurantoin is used to treat urinary tract infections (UTIs) such as cystitis.
It may also be prescribed to help prevent UTIs.
How to take nitrofurantoin
Nitrofurantoin usually comes as capsules, tablets and a liquid you swallow.
How much you need to take and for how long depends on why you're taking it. Most people need to take it for 3 to 7 days.
Nitrofurantoin can also be prescribed for long-term use if you get UTIs that keep coming back, or as a single-use medicine. You may only need to use it once to prevent a UTI before surgery, for example, or before coming into contact with a known trigger.
When taking nitrofurantoin:
the dose will depend on the type of capsule or tablet you're taking – some are slow release (prolonged release) which means you do not have to take them as often
swallow the tablets or capsules whole – do not break or chew them
try to space your doses evenly throughout the day if you're taking more than 1 dose a day
use the syringe or spoon that comes with the liquid medicine to measure the right dose
you can take it with food or a glass of milk – this makes it easier for the medicine to work
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
If you're taking nitrofurantoin for an infection you already have, it's important to keep taking your medicine until you've finished it all, even if you start to feel better.
If you miss a dose of nitrofurantoin
If you forget to take a dose of nitrofurantoin, take it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for your next dose.
If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses to make up for a forgotten dose.
If you take too much nitrofurantoin
Taking an extra dose of nitrofurantoin is unlikely to cause problems, but it's important to get medical advice if you take too much as it can sometimes be dangerous.
Urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist as soon as possible if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of nitrofurantoin
If you're unable to speak to a pharmacist, or you took too much of your medicine on purpose, call NHS 111 for advice.

If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.

Side effects of nitrofurantoin
Like all medicines, nitrofurantoin can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Side effects
Side effects of nitrofurantoin can include:
nausea (feeling sick)
diarrhoea
loss of appetite
headaches
turning your pee a dark yellow or brownish colour
Side effects usually go away when you stop taking the medicine.
Nitrofurantoin will not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how to reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you’re advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Nitrofurantoin can sometimes cause serious side effects.
Possible serious side effects of nitrofurantoin include:
lung problems – causing symptoms such as a cough, coughing up blood or mucus and breathing problems
liver problems such as jaundice and hepatitis – causing yellowing of the eyes and skin (this may be harder to see on black or brown skin)
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
blood problems – causing symptoms such as bruising or bleeding you cannot explain (including nosebleeds), sore throat, a high temperature and feeling tired or generally unwell
severe headaches
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking nitrofurantoin and might be having anaphylaxis
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.
More about side effects
These are not all the side effects of nitrofurantoin.

Who can and cannot take nitrofurantoin
Most adults and children over 3 months old can take nitrofurantoin.
But nitrofurantoin is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've previously had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from nitrofurantoin
you have a lung condition
you have severe kidney problems
you have diabetes
you have anaemia or vitamin B deficiency
you have a condition that affects your blood called porphyria
Before taking nitrofurantoin, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and nitrofurantoin
You can usually take nitrofurantoin while breastfeeding, but check with a pharmacist or doctor first.
It's rare for breastfed babies to get any side effects from medicine you're taking, but speak to a healthcare professional if you have any concerns about your baby or call NHS 111.
Pregnancy and nitrofurantoin
You can usually take nitrofurantoin before week 28 of pregnancy.
A doctor may not recommend nitrofurantoin after week 28 of pregnancy because it can affect your baby's red blood cells, but they will talk to you about the benefits and risks.
Nitrofurantoin is the best medicine to treat a UTI and it's important to treat them if you're pregnant.
Find out more about taking nitrofurantoin during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy) website
Taking nitrofurantoin with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with nitrofurantoin.
This includes some types of:
medicines to treat indigestion, such as antacids
medicines to treat certain infections, such as quinolones
medicines, such as acetazolamide, to treat glaucoma
medicines to treat cystitis that you buy from a pharmacy
Before taking nitrofurantoin, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with nitrofurantoin
You can eat and drink as normal while taking nitrofurantoin. It's best to take nitrofurantoin with food.
