Key points
You can get sick eating food or drinking water that is contaminated with the Cyclospora parasite.
Follow safe food handling practices to reduce your risk of illness.
Stay up to date on food safety alerts. Do not eat recalled food.
Overview
If you eat food or drink water that is contaminated with Cyclospora, you could get a gastrointestinal illness called cyclosporiasis. Follow the prevention steps below to reduce your risk of illness. Keep current on food safety alerts and contact your healthcare provider if you are experiencing watery diarrhea or other symptoms of cyclosporiasis.
Prevention tips
Actions to take
Wash your hands.
Wash your hands with soap and water before and after handling or preparing raw fruits and vegetables.
Wash all fruits and vegetables.
Wash all fruits and vegetables thoroughly under running water before eating, cutting, or cooking.
Scrub firm produce with a clean brush.
Scrub firm fruits and vegetables like melons and cucumbers with a clean produce brush under running water.
Cut away damaged or bruised areas.
Cut away any damaged or bruised areas on fruits and vegetables.
Cook produce when possible.
Cook produce to a temperature of at least 158 °F (70°C), since washing alone can't guarantee the parasite's removal.
Refrigerate produce as soon as possible.
Refrigerate cut, peeled, or cooked fruits and vegetables as soon as possible (within two hours).
Stay up to date on food safety alerts.
Keep current with food safety alerts and throw out recalled food or return it to the store.
Keep in mind
If you are traveling to tropical or subtropical areas where cyclosporiasis is regularly occurring, it's important to know that routine treatment of food or water is unlikely to kill Cyclospora.
