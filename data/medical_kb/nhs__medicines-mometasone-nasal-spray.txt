Mometasone nasal spray
- Common brands: Clarinaze, Nasonex
Mometasone nasal spray is a steroid medicine used to treat allergic rhinitis and nasal polyps. You can buy it from shops and pharmacies if you’re aged 18 years or older. For younger people, and for treating polyps, it's only available with a prescription.
Mometasone also comes as an inhaler, cream, ointment and lotion to treat other conditions.
What mometasone nasal spray is for
Mometasone nasal spray is a steroid medicine.
It eases swelling and irritation in your nose and can help with symptoms including an itchy nose, sneezing and a blocked or runny nose.
You can take it to treat and prevent hay fever and other allergies like a pet allergy.
For painless, soft growths inside your nose (nasal polyps), mometasone can reduce swelling and shrink the size of the growths.
There’s also a prescription-only nasal spray that contains mometasone combined with an antihistamine.
How to use mometasone nasal spray
Mometasone comes as a liquid that you spray into your nose (nasal spray). You’ll usually use it once a day.
The usual starting dose:
for adults and children aged 12 years and older: 2 sprays in each nostril
for children aged 3 to 11 years: 1 spray in each nostril
Once your symptoms have improved, you may be able to reduce your dose.
For allergic rhinitis, this medicine takes a few days to work, so keep using it even if your symptoms do not get better immediately.
If you’re using it to treat nasal polyps, it may take several weeks for your symptoms to improve. Speak to your doctor if you feel no better after 5 to 6 weeks.
Do not use the spray more often than it says in the instructions. If you bought it in a shop or pharmacy, do not have more than 4 sprays in 24 hours. If you have it on prescription, follow the advice from your doctor.
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems using it.
How to use a nasal spray
If you’re using a new bottle, or you’ve not used it for a while, pump the spray a few times until a fine mist comes out of the top of the bottle (the nozzle).
Shake the bottle and remove the cap.
Gently blow your nose.
Push your finger against the side of one nostril.
Tilt your head forward slightly and, keeping the bottle upright, put the nozzle in the other nostril.
Start to breathe in slowly through your nose.
Spray the mist into your nostril and breathe it in.
Breathe out through your mouth.
Repeat with your other nostril.
Wipe the nozzle with a clean tissue and replace the cap.
Clean the nozzle and cap once a week, or more often if the spray gets blocked.
Important:
How long to use it for
If you have a prescription for mometasone nasal spray, do not stop using it unless your doctor tells you to. If you stop using it, your symptoms may come back.
If you bought mometasone nasal spray from a shop or pharmacy, check the packet or leaflet to see how long to use it for and when to talk to a doctor if your symptoms do not improve or get worse.
If you miss a dose of mometasone nasal spray
If you forget to take a dose of mometasone nasal spray, use it as soon as you remember, unless it’s almost time for your next dose. In this case, skip the missed dose and take the next one at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses to make up for a forgotten one.
If you use too much mometasone nasal spray
Do not worry if you sometimes use more mometasone nasal spray than you should. But avoid regularly using too much because it can increase the risk of side effects.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist if:
you've used more mometasone nasal spray than it says on the packet, leaflet or prescription and it’s causing any symptoms

Side effects of mometasone nasal spray
Like all medicines, mometasone nasal spray can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of mometasone nasal spray include:
headache
a dry or painful nose or throat
nosebleeds
Mometasone nasal spray will not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery, but some people may get blurred vision when using it. If this happens to you, wait until this has gone away.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you’re advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Mometasone nasal spray can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of mometasone nasal spray include:
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
glaucoma or cataracts – causing problems with your eyes such as blurred vision, double vision or eye pain
small holes in the cartilage that separates your nostrils (your septum) – causing symptoms such as whistling when breathing through your nose or a change in the shape of your nose
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're using mometasone nasal spray and think you might be having anaphylaxis
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

Who can and cannot use mometasone nasal spray
Most adults can use mometasone nasal spray. It's sometimes also prescribed for children aged 3 years or older.
But mometasone nasal spray is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from mometasone nasal spray or another steroid (corticosteroid)
you have a nasal infection that is not currently being treated
you recently had nasal surgery
you have tuberculosis (TB)
Before using mometasone nasal spray, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and mometasone nasal spray
You can usually use mometasone nasal spray while breastfeeding, but if you are buying it without a prescription check with a pharmacist or doctor first.
It's rare for breastfed babies to get any side effects from medicine you're taking, but speak to a healthcare professional if you have any concerns about your baby.
Pregnancy and mometasone nasal spray
Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or trying to get pregnant.
They may suggest other ways to manage your symptoms, or they may say it’s OK to use mometasone nasal spray while pregnant.
If you are planning to buy mometasone nasal spray without a prescription, speak to a pharmacist or doctor first.
Find out more about treating allergic rhinitis during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy) website
Using mometasone nasal spray with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with mometasone nasal spray.
These include:
other steroid medicines (corticosteroids), particularly steroid injections or steroid tablets
some medicines for HIV, including ritonavir and cobicistat
Before using mometasone nasal spray, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with mometasone nasal spray
You can eat and drink as normal while using mometasone nasal spray.
