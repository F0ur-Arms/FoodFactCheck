At a glance
The Office of Tribal Affairs (OTA) has a mission to affirm the government-to-government relationship between CDC/ATSDR and AI/AN tribes by advancing connections and providing expertise to improve tribal communities’ health.
Federal-Tribal Relationship
The United States has a unique legal and political relationship with American Indian and Alaska Native tribal nations (AI/AN) as provided in the Constitution of the United States, treaties, and federal statutes.
The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry (CDC/ATSDR) are committed to working with federally recognized tribal governments on a government-to-government basis and strongly support and respect sovereignty and self-determination for tribal governments in the United States.
OTA focuses on activities that reflect the agency's role in helping to ensure that AI/AN communities receive public health services that keep them safe and healthy.
Our mission is to affirm the government-to-government relationship between CDC/ATSDR and the 575 federally recognized AI/AN tribal nations (variously called tribes, nations, bands, pueblos, communities, and native villages) in the United States by advancing connections and providing expertise to improve tribal communities’ health.
Our Work
Serves as principal advisor to policy-level officials on AI/AN public health matters
Be the primary contact for public health activities impacting Indian Country
Coordinates CDC/ATSDR programs for AI/AN tribal nations
Manages the CDC/ATSDR Tribal Advisory Committee
Develops communication resources to educate and guide CDC's tribal-related partnerships and activities
