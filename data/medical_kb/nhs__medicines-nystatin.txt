Nystatin
Nystatin is a medicine mainly used to treat and prevent oral thrush. It's only available with a prescription.
What nystatin is for
Nystatin is an antifungal medicine. It's used to treat or prevent thrush or other fungal infections in your mouth or throat.
There are also creams and ointments that contain nystatin and a steroid or antibiotic medicine. These are used to treat nappy rash and other skin conditions.
How to take nystatin
Nystatin comes as a liquid you hold in your mouth and then swallow (oral suspension).
To treat oral thrush, you'll usually need to take the liquid 4 times a day for 7 days, and then for 2 days after your symptoms have gone.
When taking nystatin liquid:
shake the bottle well and use the syringe or cup that comes with it to make sure you measure the correct dose
try to hold the liquid in your mouth for 30 to 60 seconds before swallowing, if possible – this will help the medicine work better, but do not worry if you swallow it quicker than this
try to space the doses equally throughout the day
it's best to take it after food
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
If you miss a dose of nystatin
If you forget to take a dose of nystatin, take it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for your next dose.
If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses to make up for a missed dose.
If you take too much nystatin
Do not worry if you sometimes use more nystatin than you should. But avoid regularly using too much because it can increase the risk of side effects.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of nystatin and it's causing any symptoms

Side effects of nystatin
Like all medicines, nystatin can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Possible side effects of nystatin include:
stomach pain, discomfort or bloating
indigestion
diarrhoea
feeling sick (nausea) and being sick (vomiting)
Nystatin will not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Nystatin can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of nystatin include:
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash and difficulty breathing
angioedema – causing swelling in areas such as your lips, tongue and face
a severe skin reaction (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) – causing flu-like symptoms, a rash, blisters and sores
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking nystatin and think you're having symptoms of a severe skin reaction, angioedema or anaphylaxis
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

Who can and cannot take nystatin
Most adults and children can take nystatin. It's also sometimes prescribed for newborn babies.
But nystatin is not suitable for some people. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from nystatin or other antifungal medicine
you have a fungal infection in your lungs or on your skin
Check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine to see if it's suitable for you. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you're not sure if you can take or use it.
Breastfeeding and nystatin
Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you're breastfeeding. They may recommend using nystatin if the benefits outweigh the risks.
If you're taking nystatin while breastfeeding, speak to a healthcare professional if you have any concerns about your baby.
Pregnancy and nystatin
Speak to a doctor or pharmacist before taking nystatin if you're pregnant or planning to get pregnant.
They may recommend using nystatin if the benefits outweigh the risks.
Taking nystatin with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with nystatin.
Check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine to see if it's suitable for you. Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if you're not sure you can take it.
Food, drink and alcohol with nystatin
You can eat and drink as normal while taking nystatin.
