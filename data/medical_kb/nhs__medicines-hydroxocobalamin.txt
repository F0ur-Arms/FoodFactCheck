Hydroxocobalamin
Hydroxocobalamin is a vitamin that's used to prevent and treat vitamin B12 deficiency. It's only available on prescription.
What hydroxocobalamin is for
Hydroxocobalamin is a man-made (artificial) version of vitamin B12. It's used to treat and prevent vitamin B12 deficiency, which happens when you have low levels of this vitamin in your body.
Your body needs vitamin B12 to make red blood cells. Red blood cells carry oxygen around the body.
You can get vitamin B12 from your food or supplements, but sometimes you might not be able to get enough. This can happen if you have a condition that affects how your body absorbs vitamin B12, you have a vegan diet, or you do not eat much meat or dairy products.
How hydroxocobalamin is given
Hydroxocobalamin comes as an injection. Your GP can give you the injection, but sometimes you might have it in hospital.
You'll usually start by having a few injections a week. As your symptoms get better, you might only have one every 2 to 3 months.
You may be able to stop having hydroxocobalamin injections when your vitamin B12 blood levels are back to normal. However, you may need to have them for the rest of your life if you cannot absorb enough vitamin B12 from your food or because of a condition.
If you miss a dose of hydroxocobalamin
If you miss your injection, the level of vitamin B12 in your body may go down further. This may make your symptoms worse.
Ask your doctor when you will have your next dose.
Side effects of hydroxocobalamin
Like all medicines, hydroxocobalamin can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Side effects of hydroxocobalamin include:
pain, swelling or itchy skin where you had the injection
feeling or being sick (nausea or vomiting)
diarrhoea
headaches
dizziness
hot flushes
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Hydroxocobalamin can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of hydroxocobalamin include:
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
irregular heartbeats (arrhythmia) or heartbeats that suddenly become more noticeable (palpitations)
skin reactions – causing symptoms such as a rash with blisters, feeling faint, wheezing or difficulty breathing
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking hydroxocobalamin and think you might be having anaphylaxis
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.
More about side effects
These are not all the side effects of hydroxocobalamin.
For a full list, see the leaflet that comes with your medicine or search for your medicine leaflet on the electronic medicines compendium website.
You can report any side effects you get on the Yellow Card website
Who can and cannot have hydroxocobalamin
Most adults and children can have hydroxocobalamin injections.
But hydroxocobalamin is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from hydroxocobalamin
you're allergic to cobalt
Before having hydroxocobalamin, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and hydroxocobalamin
You may be able to have hydroxocobalamin injections while you're breastfeeding, but check with a pharmacist or doctor first.
It's rare for breastfed babies to get any side effects from medicine you're taking, but speak to a healthcare professional if you have any concerns about your baby or call NHS 111.
Pregnancy and hydroxocobalamin
You can usually have hydroxocobalamin injections if you're pregnant, but check with a pharmacist or doctor first.
Find out more about taking hydroxocobalamin during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy) website
Having hydroxocobalamin with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with hydroxocobalamin, including oral contraceptives.
Before having hydroxocobalamin injections, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with hydroxocobalamin
You can eat and drink as normal while having hydroxocobalamin.
