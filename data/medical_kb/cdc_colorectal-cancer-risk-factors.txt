Key points
Your risk of getting colorectal cancer increases as you get older.
Personal choices and certain health conditions can also raise your risk
Age and lifestyle
Your risk of getting colorectal cancer increases as you get older. About 93% of colorectal cancers are found in people who are 45 or older.
Your lifestyle may increase your colorectal cancer risk, such as:
Not getting enough physical activity.
Not keeping a healthy weight.
Drinking alcohol.
Using tobacco.
Conditions that can increase risk
Certain health conditions can also increase your chance of getting colorectal cancer, such as:
Inflammatory bowel disease such as Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis.
A personal or family history of colorectal cancer or colorectal polyps.
A genetic syndrome such as familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP) or hereditary non-polyposis colorectal cancer (Lynch syndrome).
Being overweight or having obesity.
