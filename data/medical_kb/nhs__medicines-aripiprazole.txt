Aripiprazole
- Common brand: Abilify
Aripiprazole is a medicine used to treat schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. It's only available with a prescription.
What aripiprazole is for
Aripiprazole is a medicine called an antipsychotic.
It can be used to:
treat schizophrenia and psychosis
treat and prevent extremely high moods (mania) in people with bipolar disorder
How to take aripiprazole
Aripiprazole can be taken as:
tablets that you swallow with water
tablets that melt on your tongue (orodispersible)
a liquid (oral solution) that you swallow – this comes with a spoon or syringe for you to measure the correct dose
Take your aripiprazole at the same time each day. It can be taken with or without food, but do not mix the liquid medicine into other food or drink.
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
Treatment with aripiprazole is usually long-term. You’ll usually need a check-up every year to make sure the medicine is still suitable for you.
Sometimes you may be given aripiprazole as an injection. A nurse or doctor will give you the injection every 1 or 2 months.
It's important to keep taking your aripiprazole, even if you feel better. If you stop taking it, your symptoms may come back. Always follow your doctor's advice.
If you miss a dose of aripiprazole
If you forget to take a dose of aripiprazole, take it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for your next dose.
If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses to make up for a missed dose.
If you take too much aripiprazole
Taking an extra dose of aripiprazole can be dangerous.
Urgent advice: Call NHS 111 if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of aripiprazole
If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.
Bring the aripiprazole packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.
How to stop taking aripiprazole
Keep taking aripiprazole for as long as your doctor advises you to.
Do not stop taking it suddenly because it may cause withdrawal symptoms.
If your doctor advises you to stop taking it, your dose will usually need to be reduced gradually. Always follow your doctor's advice.
Side effects of aripiprazole
Like all medicines, aripiprazole can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of aripiprazole include:
headaches
feeling dizzy
feeling sleepy or very tired, or having difficulty sleeping
difficulty pooing (constipation)
indigestion, feeling sick (nausea) and being sick (vomiting)
feeling anxious or restless
blurred vision
If you feel sleepy, dizzy or have blurred vision after taking aripiprazole, do not drive, ride a bike or use machinery until you no longer have these symptoms.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a GP or pharmacist if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Aripiprazole can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of aripiprazole include:
high blood sugar and diabetes – symptoms include feeling very thirsty, needing to pee a lot, feeling very tired and losing weight without trying
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – which can cause symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
a fast heartbeat, sweating, shaking, muscle stiffness and feeling confused or agitated – these are signs of serious conditions called serotonin syndrome or neuroleptic malignant syndrome
feeling unable to stay still, or uncontrollable jerking or twisting movements
impulse control problems, such as an urge to gamble, binge eat, shop uncontrollably, or engage in risky sexual behaviour
very low mood and having suicidal thoughts
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking aripiprazole and think you might be having anaphylaxis
If you're having thoughts of harming yourself, or you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.
More about side effects
These are not all the side effects of aripiprazole.
For a full list, see the leaflet that comes with your medicine or search for your medicine leaflet on the electronic medicines compendium website.

Who can and cannot take aripiprazole
Most adults can take aripiprazole.
It's sometimes also prescribed for children aged 13 years or older.
But aripiprazole is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from aripiprazole or another antipsychotic medicine
you or someone in your family has diabetes
you or someone in your family has heart disease
you've ever had a blood clot or a stroke
you have epilepsy or another condition that can cause seizures (fits)
you've ever had a problem with gambling
Before taking aripiprazole, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and aripiprazole
Tell your doctor if you're breastfeeding. Aripiprazole can sometimes be used while breastfeeding if the benefits outweigh the risks.
It's rare for breastfed babies to get any side effects of medicines you're using, but speak to a healthcare professional or call NHS 111 if:
your baby is unusually sleepy or restless
your baby is not feeding as usual or not gaining weight as expected
Do not sleep in the same bed (co-sleep) with your baby if you've taken aripiprazole or any other medicine that makes you sleepy or drowsy, as it can increase the risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS).
Pregnancy and aripiprazole
Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or you get pregnant while taking aripiprazole.
Aripiprazole can be used during pregnancy if needed. Your doctor will talk to you about the risks and benefits of taking it while you're pregnant.
Your baby may need extra checks during pregnancy, and you'll usually be advised to give birth in hospital so your baby can be closely monitored after you've given birth.
Find out more about taking aripiprazole during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy) website
Taking aripiprazole with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with aripiprazole.
These include:
antidepressants
medicines to lower your blood pressure
some medicines for fungal infections
some medicines for HIV
some epilepsy medicines, such as carbamazepine
medicines for migraines such as sumatriptan and amitriptyline
medicines that can make you sleepy, such as promethazine
strong painkillers such as tramadol
the herbal remedy St John's wort
Before taking aripiprazole, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with aripiprazole
You can eat and drink as normal while taking aripiprazole, but it's best not to drink alcohol because it can increase the risk of side effects.
