Tibolone
- Common brands: Livial
Tibolone is a medicine used to treat symptoms that can happen after the menopause (when your periods stop). It’s only available with a prescription.
What tibolone is for
Tibolone is a type of hormone replacement therapy (HRT) that’s used to help with symptoms caused by the menopause, including:
a hot face, neck and chest (hot flushes)
vaginal symptoms
low mood
It's also sometimes prescribed to help prevent weakening of the bones (osteoporosis) after the menopause.
Although some types of HRT can be used while your periods are still changing (perimenopause), tibolone is usually only recommended if it’s been at least 12 months since your periods stopped.
How to take tibolone
Tibolone comes as tablets that you take once a day.
Take 1 tablet a day at the same time each day. Swallow the tablet whole with some water.
The pack containing the tablets will usually be labelled with days of the week and arrows to help you make sure you take your tablets on the right day.
Check the instructions to make sure you take your medicine correctly.
Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
If you miss a dose of tibolone
If you forget to take a dose of tibolone, take it as soon as you remember, unless it’s more than 12 hours since you were supposed to take it.
If it’s more than 12 hours later, skip the missed dose and take the next one at the usual time.
If you take too much tibolone
Taking an extra dose of tibolone is unlikely to cause problems, but it’s important to get medical advice if you take too much as it can sometimes be dangerous.
Urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist as soon as possible if:
you’ve taken more than your prescribed dose of tibolone and you have symptoms
If you’re unable to speak to a pharmacist, or you took too much of your medicine on purpose, call NHS 111 for advice.
Find a pharmacy
If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.
Bring the tibolone packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.
Side effects of tibolone
Like all medicines, tibolone can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of tibolone include:
pain in your breasts, stomach or pelvis
vaginal itching, irritation or bleeding
unusual hair growth
weight gain
Tibolone does not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a doctor or pharmacist if:
you have any side effects that are bothering you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how to reduce any side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you’re advised to stop.
Serious side effects
Tibolone can sometimes cause more serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of tibolone include:
sudden, severe headaches (similar to migraine headaches)
pain and swelling in your legs (this may be a sign of deep vein thrombosis), or sudden chest pain or difficulty breathing (these may be signs of a pulmonary embolism)
chest pain or severe difficulty breathing – these may be signs of a heart attack
weakness on 1 side of the face (the mouth or eye may droop), difficulty raising the arms and keeping them raised, difficulty speaking (speech may be slurred) – these may be symptoms of a stroke
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash and difficulty breathing
There’s also an increased risk of getting breast cancer or womb cancer if you take tibolone, particularly if you take it for a long time. Your doctor will discuss the risks and benefits with you.
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking tibolone and think you might be having anaphylaxis, a pulmonary embolism, heart attack or stroke
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.
More about side effects
These are not all the side effects of tibolone.
For a full list, see the leaflet that came with your medicine, or search for your medicine leaflet on the electronic medicines compendium website.
You can report any side effects you get on the Yellow Card Scheme website
Who can and cannot take tibolone
Tibolone is only recommended after the menopause if symptoms such as hot flushes and vaginal problems are affecting your daily life.
You'll usually need to wait at least 12 months after your last period before taking tibolone.
Sometimes it might be possible to start taking it straight away. Your doctor will be able to advise you.
Tibolone is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or serious side effect from tibolone
you have or have had breast cancer
you have a condition that affects the lining of the womb, such as womb cancer or endometrial hyperplasia (when the womb lining becomes too thick)
you have a heart condition, such as angina, heart attack or stroke
you have a liver condition like liver disease
you have or have had a blood clot in your legs (DVT) or in your lungs (pulmonary embolism)
you have any unexplained vaginal bleeding
Pregnancy and breastfeeding and tibolone
You should not take tibolone if you’re pregnant or breastfeeding.
Taking tibolone with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with tibolone.
These can include:
medicines to prevent blood clots (anticoagulants), such as warfarin
epilepsy medicines, such as phenobarbital, phenytoin, lamotrigine and carbamazepine
medicines used to treat tuberculosis, such as rifampicin and rifabutin
HIV medicines, such as nevirapine, efavirenz, ritonavir and nelfinavir
St John’s Wort, which is a herbal remedy that’s sometimes used to treat mild depression
Before taking tibolone, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with tibolone
You can eat and drink as normal while taking tibolone.
