Medroxyprogesterone tablets
Medroxyprogesterone tablets are medicine used to treat heavy, painful or irregular periods. They're only available with a prescription.
What medroxyprogesterone tablets are for
Medroxyprogesterone tablets are used to treat heavy, painful or irregular periods.
They're also sometimes used in the treatment of:
endometriosis
menopause symptoms, as part of hormone replacement therapy (HRT)
breast, womb (uterus) and prostate cancer
Medroxyprogesterone tablets are a type of medicine called progestogens. They work like the hormone progesterone, which your body makes.
How to take medroxyprogesterone tablets
How you take medroxyprogesterone tablets, and for how long, depends on what you're taking them for and your symptoms.
You may be advised to take them:
a few days each month, for period problems and HRT
every day for a few months, for endometriosis
Your doctor or specialist treatment team will advise you on how to take them.
When taking medroxyprogesterone tablets:
swallow them whole with a drink of water
take them at the same time every day
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take your medicine or have problems taking it.
If you miss a dose of medroxyprogesterone
If you forget to take a dose, take it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for the next dose.
If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses to make up for a forgotten dose.
If you take too much medroxyprogesterone
Taking an extra dose of medroxyprogesterone is unlikely to cause problems but can cause tummy pain, headache and vaginal bleeding. It's important to get medical advice because if you take too much, it can sometimes be dangerous.
Urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist as soon as possible if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of medroxyprogesterone tablets
If you're unable to speak to a pharmacist, or you took too much of your medicine on purpose, call NHS 111 for advice.

Side effects of medroxyprogesterone tablets
Like all medicines, medroxyprogesterone tablets can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of medroxyprogesterone tablets include:
vaginal bleeding or spotting
headaches
feeling sick (nausea)
depression or anxiety
difficulty sleeping
hair loss
itchy skin
breast pain
weight gain
Medroxyprogesterone tablets will not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you’re advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Medroxyprogesterone tablets can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of medroxyprogesterone tablets include:
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – which can cause symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
stroke – which can cause symptoms such as a severe headache, difficulty speaking, or weakness in your face or arms
a blood clot (deep vein thrombosis) – which can cause symptoms such as severe pain and swelling in your lower leg or foot, the area may be warm to touch or red or purple (which may be harder to see on black or brown skin)
pulmonary embolism (a blood clot in your lungs) – which can cause symptoms such as severe pain in your chest, sudden difficulty breathing or coughing up blood
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking medroxyprogesterone tablets and think you might be having anaphylaxis, a stroke or have a blood clot
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

Who can and cannot take medroxyprogesterone tablets
Most adults can take medroxyprogesterone tablets.
But medroxyprogesterone tablets are not suitable for everyone. For example, they may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from taking progesterone
you have migraines
you have high blood pressure
you've had or have depression
you have epilepsy
you have lupus
you have heart, liver or kidney problems
you've had or have breast cancer
you've had or have a meningioma (a type of tumour on the brain or spinal cord)
Before taking medroxyprogesterone tablets, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and medroxyprogesterone tablets
Tell your doctor if you're breastfeeding. Medroxyprogesterone tablets are not usually used while breastfeeding.
Pregnancy and medroxyprogesterone tablets
Medroxyprogesterone tablets are not used during pregnancy. Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or trying to get pregnant before you take medroxyprogesterone tablets.
Taking medroxyprogesterone tablets with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with medroxyprogesterone tablets.
This includes:
medicines used to treat seizures, such a phenobarbital
some antibiotics, such as rifampicin or rifabutin
medicines used to treat HIV, such as ritonavir
St John's wort (a herbal remedy)
Before taking medroxyprogesterone tablets, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with medroxyprogesterone tablets
You can eat and drink as normal while taking medroxyprogesterone tablets.
