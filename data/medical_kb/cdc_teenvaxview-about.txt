About
Vaccination is an essential part of keeping adolescents healthy and protected from serious diseases. Vaccines are recommended for adolescents to protect against diseases like whooping cough, meningococcal disease, and cancers caused by human papillomavirus (HPV). Adolescents should also get any vaccines they missed in childhood.
Data sources
The NIS-Teen is a mobile phone survey used to monitor relevant vaccination coverage among adolescents ages 13–17 years. It is part of a group of surveys that monitor:
vaccination coverage among children ages 19–35 months
flu vaccination for children ages 6 months–17 years
COVID-19 vaccination for children and adolescents in eligible age groups and for adults ages 18 years and older.
The NIS-Teen provides a "report card" to let us know how well we are protecting our nation's adolescents against vaccine-preventable diseases. This mobile telephone survey of parents or guardians is followed by a questionnaire mailed to vaccination providers to obtain the adolescent's' vaccination history. For data and documentation related to NIS-Teen please see NIS-Teen Data and Documentation for 2015 to Present.
Objectives, targets, and indicators
Objectives, targets, and indicators are science-based goals for adolescent vaccination coverage geared toward reducing illness and death from vaccine-preventable diseases.
Every 10 years, Healthy People provides science-based national objectives for improving the health of all Americans. The HP 2030 objective IID-08 includes specific targets on increasing HPV vaccination coverage among adolescents.
HEDIS measures are tools used by health plans to measure performance on childhood vaccinations, including childhood immunization status.
The Core Set of Children's Health Care Quality Measures for Medicaid and CHIP (Child Core Set) includes childhood immunization status measures.
Information for specific groups
Healthcare professionals play an integral role in preventing serious diseases through vaccination. You can reduce illness among adolescents by recommending appropriate vaccinations at every health visit, taking the time to answer parent's questions, implementing standing orders, and using reminder/recall systems.
Your work ensures high vaccination coverage in local communities and helps keep adolescents safe and healthy. Learn how you as a healthcare professional can protect more children in your area.
Health care professionals may also be asked to help the NIS provide accurate information about childhood vaccinations. It is very important to continue to fill out the Immunization History Questionnaire (IHQ) for CDC to track accurate vaccination coverage at national, state, and selected local levels. The survey could not be completed without their dedicated support. Find the IHQ and other materials on the National Immunization Survey site.
The diseases that vaccines prevent can be very serious—even life-threatening. That's why CDC's recommended adolescent immunization schedule is designed to protect adolescents before they are exposed to these diseases. Making sure adolescents receive all their recommended vaccines helps protect them from several serious diseases by the time they reach 18 years of age:
Tdap vaccine to protect against tetanus, diphtheria, acellular pertussis
Meningococcal conjugate (MenACWY) vaccine to protect against meningococcal disease from several serogroups
HPV vaccine to protect against human papillomavirus
Influenza (flu) vaccine to protect against seasonal flu
Meningococcal B (MenB) vaccine to project against meningococcal disease from serogroup B

The National Immunization Surveys (NIS) are conducted in households across the country by phone. The majority of the telephone numbers are randomly selected by a computer so it is possible that your phone number may be contacted. Your participation is voluntary and no information that identifies any individual is ever shared or released. All survey responses and personal data are kept confidential in accordance with strict Federal regulations. Survey results are analyzed and published in reports to benefit local, state, and national public health programs.
