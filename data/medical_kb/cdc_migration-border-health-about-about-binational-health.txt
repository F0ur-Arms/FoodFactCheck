Key points
The United States and Mexico are connected through border and binational communities with shared social, cultural, economic, and environmental conditions.
CDC works with U.S. and Mexico partners to prevent, detect, and control infectious diseases that affect communities and travelers in the border region and binational populations throughout the United States.
Overview
The United States and Mexico share strong social, cultural, and economic ties, with people routinely living, working, traveling, and maintaining connections across both countries. These connections create both opportunities and challenges for preventing and controlling infectious diseases in the border regionA and among binational communities throughout the United States and Mexico. Public health agencies from both countries work together to address shared health challenges and protect the health of their communities.
Terms to know
U.S.-Mexico border health focuses on health issues affecting people who live in or travel through the border region. These issues can be influenced by travel, migration, social and physical contact between communities, and exposure to products, animals, insects, or environmental conditions.
U.S.-Mexico binational health focuses on health issues affecting people and communities with connections to both countries, including people who live in one country and regularly travel to the other, have family or social ties across both countries, or use products from the other country.
Facts
Border crossings
There were 182.4 million northbound border crossings at official land ports of entry1 in 2025.
Tourism
Mexico is a top foreign tourist destination for U.S. travelers and the United States is the primary destination for Mexican travelers2.
In 2025, 20.7 million people traveled by air from the United States to Mexico and 20.6 million from Mexico to the United States3.
Border region residents and expatriates
According to census data, about 8.2 million people lived in the U.S. border region in 20254 and 8.8 million lived in the border region of Mexico in 20205.
Mexico is the number one country of residence for U.S.-born people living outside of the United States. An estimated 1.6 million U.S. citizens live in Mexico6.
In 2024, the United States was home to 11.1 million people born in Mexico7 and, 39 million people with Mexican heritage8, many born in the United States.
Trade
Mexico is the United States’ largest agricultural trading partner.
In 2025, the United States bought about 89% of Mexico's agricultural exports and supplied about 66% of Mexico's agricultural imports9.
What CDC is doing
CDC's Division of Global Migration Health, Southern Border Health and Migration Branch works with local, state, and federal health officials in the United States and Mexico to prevent, detect, and respond to infectious diseases that affect the border region and binational communities. This work helps protect border region communities and travelers, as well as binational and other mobile populations throughout the United States.
