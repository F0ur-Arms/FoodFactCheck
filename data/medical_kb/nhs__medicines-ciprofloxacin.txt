Ciprofloxacin
- Common brands: Cetraxal
Ciprofloxacin is an antibiotic used to treat infections. It's only available with a prescription.
What ciprofloxacin is for
Ciprofloxacin is used to treat infections, or infections that have developed complications, such as:
urinary tract infections (UTIs), such as bladder or kidney infections
sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
ear or eye infections
bone and joint infections
infection of the prostate gland (prostatitis)
Ciprofloxacin can cause rare but serious side effects, some of which can be long-term or permanent. Because of this, ciprofloxacin is often only used if other antibiotics have been used first and have not worked, or you are unable to take them due to an allergy, or similar.
How to take ciprofloxacin
Ciprofloxacin comes as tablets or a liquid that you swallow.
There are also ciprofloxacin eyedrops and eardrops used to treat some types of ear and eye infections.
Most people take ciprofloxacin for at least 5 days. You may need to take it for several weeks for some types of infection.
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
Tablets
The usual dose of ciprofloxacin tablets is 250mg to 750mg twice a day. For some infections, you might only need to take a single dose.
When taking ciprofloxacin tablets:
take your medicine the same time every day
swallow tablets whole with a glass of water
Do not take ciprofloxacin tablets with any dairy products, such as cheese or milk, or drinks with added calcium, such as plant milk. Doing so can stop the medicine from working properly.
Leave a gap of at least 2 hours between taking ciprofloxacin and having these types of food and drink.
Liquid
Liquid ciprofloxacin comes in 2 bottles:
a small bottle containing ciprofloxacin granules
a larger bottle containing a liquid solvent
You will need to empty the granules from the small bottle into the large bottle before you start using the medicine.
The medicine comes with a spoon that measures out 250mg of ciprofloxacin.
The usual dose of ciprofloxacin liquid is 250mg to 750mg twice a day.
When taking ciprofloxacin liquid:
take your medicine the same time every day
use the spoon that comes with your medicine to measure out the right dose
Do not take ciprofloxacin liquid with any dairy products, such as cheese or milk, or drinks with added calcium, such as plant milk. Doing so can stop the medicine from working properly.
Leave a gap of at least 2 hours between taking ciprofloxacin and having these types of food and drink.
Follow the instructions on the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine to make sure you're taking it correctly.
Eyedrops
Most eye infections are treated with 1 to 2 drops, given 4 times a day.
But you may need more frequent drops if your infection is bad, or you develop an ulcer on your cornea (the protective shell of the eye).
To use the eyedrops:
wash your hands
gently pull down your lower eyelid and tilt your head back
bring the bottle of drops close to your eye, but do not touch your eye or eyelid – this could spread infection
gently press on the bottom of the bottle to release a drop – you should not need to squeeze it
if a drop misses your eye, then try again
Eardrops
Ciprofloxacin eardrops come in small containers of liquid. Each container has a single dose inside.
Most people are told to take 2 doses a day.
To use the eardrops:
warm the drops by holding the a single-dose container in your hands for a few minutes
wash your hands
tilt your head sideways and bring the container up to your ear, with the open end close to your ear hole
squeeze the drops into your ear then keep your head tilted sideways for at least 5 minute, to allow the drops to work their way into your ear
discard the single-dose container after use
It's important to keep taking your medicine until you've finished it all, even if you start to feel better.
If you miss a dose of ciprofloxacin
If you forget to take a dose of ciprofloxacin take it as soon as you remember, unless it's nearly time for your next dose.
If it's nearly time for your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses to make up for a missed dose.
If you take too much ciprofloxacin
If you use more ciprofloxacin eyedrops than prescribed, you should wash your eyes with clean, lukewarm water.
Taking an extra dose of ciprofloxacin tablets, liquid or eardrops is unlikely to cause problems, but it's important to get medical advice if you take too much as it can sometimes be dangerous.
Accidently swallowing ciprofloxacin eyedrops or eardrops is also unlikely to cause problems, but you should still seek medical advice if this happens.
Urgent advice: Get help from NHS 111 if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of ciprofloxacin tablets, liquid or eardrops and you get any unexpected side effects
you've swallowed ciprofloxacin eyedrops or eardrops
your child has taken more than their prescribed dose of ciprofloxacin
You can call 111 or get help from 111 online. Call 111 if you're asking about a child under the age of 5 years.
If you're told to go to A&E, do not drive. Ask someone to drive you or call 999 and ask for an ambulance.
Bring the ciprofloxacin packet or leaflet inside it, any remaining medicine, and any other medicines you take.
Side effects of ciprofloxacin
Like all medicines, ciprofloxacin can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of ciprofloxacin tablets and liquid include:
feeling sick (nausea)
being sick (vomiting)
headache
diarrhoea
eye discomfort or pain
Common side effects of ciprofloxacin eyedrops include eye pain and redness.
It is also common for eardrops to cause ear pain or itchiness and a strange taste in your mouth.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce any side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Ciprofloxacin can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of ciprofloxacin include:
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – causing symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash and difficulty breathing
the large blood vessel that carries blood from the heart splits open (acute aortic dissection) which can cause a severe spreading chest pain, difficulty speaking, weakness on 1 side of your body and difficulty breathing
seizures (fits)
numbness and tingling in your feet and hands (peripheral neuropathy)
damage to your tendons, which can cause difficulties with walking, sudden joint pain, swelling and bruising - this side effect can develop in the first 48 hours of treatment
changes to mood, which can lead to psychosis, depression and suicidal thoughts
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking ciprofloxacin and think you might be having anaphylaxis, a seizure or an acute aortic dissection
If you're having thoughts of harming yourself, or you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

Who can and cannot take ciprofloxacin
Most adults and children can take ciprofloxacin.
But ciprofloxacin is not suitable for some people. For example, it may not be suitable if you have:
had an allergic reaction to, or serious side effect from ciprofloxacin or another antibiotic of the same type (fluoroquinolones), especially if you had any side effects involving your tendons or joints
diabetes
a condition that affects your nervous system, such as epilepsy
a heart problem, such as heart failure, or you've had a heart attack in the past
a kidney condition, such as chronic kidney disease
an abdominal aortic aneurysm, or a close relative has had one
Before taking ciprofloxacin, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and ciprofloxacin
Tell your doctor if you're breastfeeding. Ciprofloxacin is not usually used while breastfeeding because small amounts pass into breast milk.
But a doctor may recommend it if the benefits outweigh the risks.
Speak to a healthcare professional or call NHS 111 if you have any concerns about your baby, or notice side effects in your baby such as:
diarrhoea
oral thrush (mouth thrush)
a skin rash
not feeding as well as usual or unusually irritable
being sleepy or unusually tired
Pregnancy and ciprofloxacin
Tell your doctor if you're pregnant. Ciprofloxacin is not usually used during pregnancy.
There are other antibiotics recommended for use in pregnancy.
But a doctor may recommend ciprofloxacin if these other antibiotics are not suitable for you, or have not worked.
Your doctor will talk to you about the risks and benefits of taking it.
Find out more about taking ciprofloxacin during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicine in Pregnancy) website
Taking ciprofloxacin with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with ciprofloxacin.
This includes:
non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), such as ibuprofen
some types of anticoagulants (such as warfarin)
medicines that help with an irregular heart rhythm such as sotalol
some types of antidepressants
medicines used to treat psychosis, such as quetiapine
steroid medicines, such as prednisolone
tizanidine, a muscle relaxant used to treat muscle cramps, stiffness and spasms in conditions like multiple sclerosis
phenytoin, used to prevent seizures in people living with epilepsy
Before taking ciprofloxacin, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with ciprofloxacin
Leave a gap of at least 2 hours between taking ciprofloxacin tablets or liquid and eating or drinking dairy products or drinks with added calcium, such as plant milk. The calcium in these products can stop your body from taking in all the medicine.
Try to avoid drinking a lot of alcohol because it can increase the risk of side effects.
