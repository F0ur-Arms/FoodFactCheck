Oestrogen tablets, patches, gel and spray
- Common brands: Oestrogel, Evorel, Sandrena, Lenzetto, Estradot, Progynova
Oestrogen is a hormone replacement therapy (HRT) medicine used to help ease menopause symptoms. It's only available with a prescription.
This information is about oestrogen medicine. There's separate information about vaginal oestrogen.
What oestrogen is for
During menopause, your body starts to make less of a hormone called oestrogen, which can cause symptoms.
Oestrogen is used to help ease menopause symptoms such as:
hot flushes and night sweats
vaginal dryness, itching or pain
sleep problems
If you still have a womb, you will need to use oestrogen along with a hormone called progestogen. This is to help protect your womb lining.
If you've had your womb removed (a hysterectomy), you will not need to use progestogen.
How to take or use oestrogen
Oestrogen comes as tablets, patches, a gel and a spray.
When you use or take oestrogen:
how you use it, how much and for how long depends on whether you still have regular periods, your symptoms and if you still have a womb
talk to a doctor if your symptoms are not getting better – it can take time to find the right type and dose to suit you
Always follow your doctor's instructions and the instructions on the leaflet that comes with your medicine.
How to use different types of oestrogen
Tablets
When taking oestrogen tablets:
you'll usually take 1 tablet a day with a drink of water or milk
you can take them with or without food
try to take them at the same time each day
start a new pack when you finish the last pack – do not take a break in between
Patches
When using oestrogen patches
stick the patch on an area below the waist, such as your thigh or bottom – do not put it near your breasts, on top of cuts, spots or irritated skin
you'll usually change your patch once or twice a week, although it depends which brand you use
if the patch falls off before you're due to change it, replace it and try to stick to the same days you usually change your patch
start a new pack as soon as you finish one – do not leave a break between packs
Gel
Oestrogen gel comes in sachets or in a bottle with a pump dispenser. Your doctor will tell you how many pumps or sachets to use.
When using oestrogen gel:
apply it once a day to clean and dry skin – try to do it at the same time of day, if possible
do not use it on the same area of skin 2 days in a row
do not use it near your breasts or genitals – spread on your shoulders, outer arms, inner thighs or lower body
wait for at least 1 hour before washing the area or getting it wet
wait up to 5 minutes for the gel to dry before getting dressed or allowing anyone else to touch the area
If you have not had a hysterectomy, oestrogen gel is usually used with progestogen medicine as well.
If you're due to have surgery, you may need to stop using oestrogen gel 4 to 6 weeks before your operation. It can sometimes increase the risk of blood clots.
Spray
you'll usually use 1 or 2 sprays a day, your doctor will tell you how many to use – try to do it at the same time each day, if possible
spray it onto your inner arm or inner thigh, but do not rub or massage it into your skin
try to wait for 2 minutes after using the spray before getting dressed
do not wash the area for at least 1 hour
If you're due to have surgery, you may need to stop using your oestrogen medicine about 4 to 6 weeks before your operation. This is because it can sometimes increase the risk of blood clots.
Do not let someone else, especially a child, touch the area where you have sprayed or spread gel, before it's dry. If they do, they need to wash the area of their skin that touched the spray with soap and water straight away.
If you miss a dose of oestrogen
Tablets, spray and gel
If you miss a dose of oestrogen and it's more than 12 hours until your next dose, take or use it as soon as you remember.
If it's less than 12 hours until your next dose, skip the missed dose and take or use the next dose at the normal time.
If you miss a dose, you may have some bleeding or spotting.
Do not take or use 2 doses of your oestrogen to make up for a forgotten dose.
Oestrogen patches
If you forget to change your HRT patch, change it as soon as you remember. Continue the same days that you change your patch.
If you use too much oestrogen
Using too much oestrogen is unlikely to cause problems, but it's important to get medical advice if you take too much as it can sometimes be dangerous. It can also increase the risk of side effects such as breast tenderness, feeling sick (nausea) and vaginal bleeding.
Urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist if:
you've used or taken more than it says on the packet, leaflet or prescription and it's causing any symptoms
If you're unable to speak to a pharmacist, or you took too much of your medicine on purpose, call NHS 111 for advice.

How to stop using oestrogen
When you start using HRT, your GP should review your medicine after the first 3 months.
If you want to stop at any point, talk to your GP, but stopping may cause your symptoms to come back.
If you want to carry on using oestrogen, you can usually do so for long as it helps your symptoms.
Side effects of oestrogen
Like all medicines, oestrogen can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of oestrogen may include:
headaches
breast pain or tenderness
skin irritation where you stick patches
unexpected vaginal bleeding or spotting, or heavier periods than usual
Oestrogen will not usually affect your ability to drive, ride a bike or use machinery.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Oestrogen can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
pain and swelling in your legs (this may be a sign of deep vein thrombosis), or sudden chest pain or difficulty breathing (these may be signs of a pulmonary embolism)
yellowing of your eyes and skin, which may be harder to see on brown or black skin – these may be signs of jaundice
weakness on 1 side of the face (the mouth or eye may droop), difficulty raising the arms and keeping them raised, difficulty speaking (speech may be slower) – these may be symptoms of a stroke
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) – which can cause symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
There is also an increased risk of breast cancer and womb (uterus) cancer, particularly if you take oestrogen for a long time. Your GP will talk to you about the risks and benefits.
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're using oestrogen and think you might be having symptoms of a pulmonary embolism, a stroke or anaphylaxis
If you think you're having any other serious side effects, call NHS 111.

Who can and cannot use oestrogen
Most adults can use oestrogen.
But it is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from oestrogen
you've ever had breast cancer, are having tests for breast cancer or are at high risk due to family history
you have vaginal bleeding with no known cause
you've ever had deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism
Before using oestrogen tell your GP if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and oestrogen
Tell your doctor if you're breastfeeding. Oestrogen is not recommended if you're breastfeeding.
Pregnancy and oestrogen
Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or trying to get pregnant. Oestrogen is not recommended during pregnancy.
Using oestrogen with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with oestrogen.
These include:
medicines or epilepsy, such as phenytoin or carbamazepine
certain medicines for infections, such as rifampicin
medicines for HIV such as antiretrovirals
herbal remedies containing St John's wort
Before taking or using oestrogen, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with oestrogen
You can eat and drink as normal while using oestrogen.
