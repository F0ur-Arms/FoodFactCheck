Empagliflozin
Empagliflozin is a medicine used to treat type 2 diabetes, heart failure and chronic kidney disease. It’s only available with a prescription.
What empagliflozin is for
Empagliflozin can be used as a treatment for:
type 2 diabetes
heart failure
chronic kidney disease (CKD)
If you have too much sugar (glucose) in your body, empagliflozin works by getting rid of the extra sugar when you pee. It can also reduce the amount of work your heart needs to do to pump blood around the body.
If you have type 2 diabetes, your doctor may prescribe empagliflozin alongside another diabetes treatment. There are also some combination tablets, which contain empagliflozin mixed with another medicine.
How to take empagliflozin
Empagliflozin usually comes as tablets that you take once a day. Most people need to take it long term.
Swallow the tablets whole with a drink of water. You can take them with or without food, but try to take them at the same time each day.
Always check the packet or leaflet that comes with your medicine. Ask a pharmacist or doctor for advice if you're not sure how to take it or have any problems taking it.
If you’re being sick (vomiting), have diarrhoea or a high temperature while taking empagliflozin, there's a risk you'll get dehydrated. Talk to a pharmacist or doctor for advice, or follow the advice they've already given you.
It's important to keep taking your medicine. If you stop suddenly, your blood sugar (glucose) may go up. Speak to your doctor first.
If you miss a dose of empagliflozin
If you forget to take a dose of empagliflozin, take it as soon as you remember, unless it’s less than 12 hours until your next dose.
If it’s less than 12 hours until your next dose, skip the dose you missed and take your next dose at the usual time.
Do not take 2 doses at the same time to make up for a forgotten one.
If you take too much empagliflozin
Taking an extra dose of empagliflozin is unlikely to cause problems, but it’s important to get medical advice if you take too much as it can sometimes be dangerous.
Urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist as soon as possible if:
you've taken more than your prescribed dose of empagliflozin
If you're unable to speak to a pharmacist, or you took too much of your medicine on purpose, call NHS 111 for advice.

Side effects of empagliflozin
Like all medicines, empagliflozin can cause side effects, although not everyone gets them.
Common side effects
Common side effects of empagliflozin include:
thrush
needing to pee more than usual
feeling more thirsty than usual
skin rash – the rash may be itchy or raised and your skin may look red or a different colour to usual
If you take empagliflozin alongside another diabetic medicine, it may cause low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia, or a hypo). This can make you feel dizzy, feel sick (nauseous), be sick (vomit), feel very hungry or very tired.
If you get symptoms of low blood sugar there are things you can do to treat it yourself. If this happens often, see your GP.
Empagliflozin should not affect your ability to drive, but if you have symptoms of a hypo – such as feeling sleepy, dizziness or blurred vision – do not drive, ride a bike or use machinery until you no longer have these symptoms.
Non-urgent advice: Speak to a pharmacist or doctor if:
you have any side effects that bother you or do not go away
They may be able to give you advice about how you can reduce the side effects. Keep taking your medicine unless you're advised to stop.

Serious side effects
Empagliflozin can sometimes cause serious side effects, although most of them are rare.
Possible serious side effects of empagliflozin include:
an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) or angioedema – which cause symptoms such as a swollen throat or tongue, a raised, itchy rash or difficulty breathing
diabetic ketoacidosis, a condition that makes you feel very thirsty, confused, unusually tired or causes breath that smells sweet
severe urinary tract infections (UTIs) – causing symptoms such as a high temperature, feeling shivery or a burning sensation when you pee
a serious infection that affects the vagina and penis (Fournier's gangrene) – causing symptoms such as severe pain, swelling, redness and blisters
Immediate action required: Call 999 if:
you're taking empagliflozin and think you might be having anaphylaxis, diabetic ketoacidosis or Fournier's gangrene
If you think you're having any other serious side effect, call NHS 111.

Who can and cannot take empagliflozin
Most adults can take empagliflozin.
But empagliflozin is not suitable for everyone. For example, it may not be suitable if:
you've had an allergic reaction or other serious side effect from empagliflozin or a similar medicine such as dapagliflozin
you have type 1 diabetes
you have diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or you're at higher risk of getting DKA
you have low blood pressure (hypotension)
you have kidney or liver problems
you often get urinary tract infections (UTIs)
Before taking empagliflozin, tell your doctor if you have any medical conditions.
Breastfeeding and empagliflozin
Empagliflozin is not usually used when breastfeeding. Your doctor may recommend a different medicine to treat your condition.
If you have diabetes, it’s important that it’s managed while you're breastfeeding.
Pregnancy and empagliflozin
Tell your doctor if you're pregnant or trying to get pregnant. They may want to switch you to a different medicine to treat your condition, as empagliflozin is not usually taken during pregnancy.
If you have diabetes, it's important that it's well controlled if you're pregnant, both for your health and to prevent any complications.
Find out more about taking antidiabetic drugs, including empagliflozin, during pregnancy on the Bumps (Best Use of Medicines in Pregnancy) website
Taking empagliflozin with other medicines, food and drink
There are some medicines, remedies and supplements that may not mix well with empagliflozin.
This includes:
medicines that make you pee more (diuretics)
medicines that lower your blood pressure
epilepsy medicines, such as phenytoin
some antibiotics, such as rifampicin for treating tuberculosis (TB)
Before taking empagliflozin, tell your doctor if you're taking any other medicines, herbal remedies, vitamins or supplements.
Food, drink and alcohol with empagliflozin
You can eat and drink as normal while taking empagliflozin.
If you have type 2 diabetes, follow the general dietary advice you've been given to help control your diabetes.
Try to limit how much alcohol you drink while taking empagliflozin as this can increase the risk of side effects like diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).
